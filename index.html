The Problem

Why It Matters

According to the Philippine Rice Research Institute (PhilRice), the Philippines produces 11–18 million tons of rice straw annually — much of which is underutilized or burned by farmers to clear fields, contributing to air pollution and wasted resources.

Many art materials today are expensive and not eco-friendly because they are made from wood, plastic, or chemically processed paper. Some artists, especially students, struggle to afford quality canvas paper and materials.

Farm by-products like rice straw are largely left in the fields or burned — seen as waste with little value. Meanwhile, eco-friendly art material options remain scarce and inaccessible.

Likha-Ani transforms agricultural waste into sustainable art materials, giving new life to farm by-products through creativity, innovation, and eco-friendly design — benefiting farmers, artists, and the planet alike.
